Multi-Objective and Multi-Task Optimization for Robust AI


February 22–23, 2027, Montréal, Canada

Call for Participation

Overview

We invite researchers, practitioners, and students to participate in the AAAI-27 Workshop on Multi-Objective and Multi-Task Optimization for Robust AI, a one-day workshop at the Forty-First AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence in Montréal, Canada. The workshop will take place on February 22 or 23, 2027; the exact date will be assigned by AAAI.

Trustworthy AI is inherently multi-dimensional. Modern AI systems must remain accurate and useful while also being robust to adversarial perturbations and distribution shift, safe, fair, privacy-preserving, transparent, reliable, and efficient. These requirements often interact or conflict, and a single scalar score can obscure the trade-offs that matter for training, evaluation, and deployment.

The workshop connects multi-objective optimization (MOO), evolutionary computation, multitask learning, and robust and trustworthy AI. We will explore how Pareto-aware optimization, hard constraints, preference elicitation, and knowledge transfer across tasks can address competing trustworthiness requirements in foundation models, reinforcement learning, and autonomous systems.

The workshop builds on the research agenda of the NeurIPS 2026 Self-Evolving Diversity-Driven Search for Robust AI Systems (EvoRobust) workshop, with a central focus on multi-objective and multi-task optimization. We welcome theory, algorithms, applications, and evaluations, including related work that motivates new optimization formulations. Contributions from early-career researchers and across disciplines are particularly encouraged.

Topics of Interest

Topics include, but are not limited to:

Multi-objective formulations for trustworthy AI
joint objectives and constraints over accuracy, robustness, safety, fairness, privacy, calibration, interpretability, latency, energy, and cost; many-objective learning; uncertainty-aware objectives; and hard trustworthiness constraints.
MOO algorithms for robust and trustworthy learning
gradient-based, evolutionary, decomposition-based, preference-guided, constrained, bilevel, Bayesian, surrogate-assisted, multi-fidelity, transfer, and multi-task MOO for training, fine-tuning, evaluation, and deployment.
Evolutionary multitasking and transfer optimization
knowledge transfer across tasks, adaptive task relatedness, multi-task search, transfer evolutionary optimization, and robust transfer under heterogeneous or changing objectives.
Robust and adversarial learning
adversarial attacks and defenses, certified robustness, distribution shift, out-of-distribution generalization, stress testing, adaptive attackers, and studies exposing hidden robustness trade-offs.
Foundation-model alignment and safety
multi-objective preference optimization; safety–helpfulness, jailbreak–over-refusal, privacy–utility, and robustness–capability trade-offs; Pareto-aware fine-tuning, model merging, red teaming, and multimodal robustness.
Agentic and autonomous AI
safety-constrained planning and reinforcement learning, tool-use and memory robustness, attacker–defender co-evolution, multi-agent robustness, and Pareto-aware long-horizon adaptation.
Responsible AI, evaluation, and deployment
fairness–accuracy and privacy–utility optimization; Pareto-aware benchmarks, metrics, and stress tests; preference elicitation; deployment-point selection; negative results; and reproducibility studies.

Submission Guidelines

We invite the following types of submissions:

  • Full research papers: up to 8 pages of main content, with references not counting toward the page limit.
  • Short, position, and work-in-progress papers: up to 4 pages of main content, with references not counting toward the page limit. We especially welcome well-motivated position papers, negative results, benchmark/evaluation papers, and reproducibility studies.

These page limits are specific to this workshop. Figures, tables, and any appendices included in the paper count toward the main-content limit; references do not.

Submissions must be in English, use the AAAI-27 two-column format, and be anonymized for double-blind review. Omit author names, affiliations, acknowledgments, and identifying links, and cite your own work in the third person. The AAAI-27 Author Kit is linked from the AAAI-27 conference website. Papers must be submitted as PDF files through the workshop submission site: [SUBMISSION SITE URL].

Publication Policy

This workshop is non-archival. Under AAAI workshop policy, AAAI does not publish workshop papers in the AAAI Digital Library or produce workshop technical reports. Accepted papers will instead be made available on the workshop website, subject to author consent. Authors remain responsible for complying with the multiple-submission and prior-publication policies of any other venue to which they submit their work.

Presentation and Participation

The workshop will be held fully in person. At least one author of each accepted paper must register for the workshop and present it in person as an oral presentation or poster, as assigned by the organizers. Virtual presentations are not permitted.

Workshop Format

The one-day program will combine invited talks, contributed oral presentations, an extended poster session, and a panel discussion. The program will alternate methodological perspectives in multi-objective and multi-task optimization with applications in robust and trustworthy AI, while reserving substantial time for submitted work and interaction.

The planned panel, What Belongs on the Pareto Front—and What Must Never Be Traded Away?, will discuss which trustworthiness properties should be optimized and which should be treated as hard constraints; when scalarization hides unacceptable failure modes; how preferences should be specified; and how a single deployable operating point should be chosen from an evolving Pareto set.
